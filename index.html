Pricing the slow road

Human drivers always take the fastest road. People hailing self-driving rides pick a route by time and price, or walk. Set the prices and watch congestion move.

A simplified version of Bıyık, Lazar, Pedarsani & Sadigh, “Incentivizing Efficient Equilibria in Traffic Networks with Mixed Autonomy” (2021). Three parallel roads use the paper’s traffic equations: self-driving cars follow more closely, so roads hold more of them before congesting. Human drivers settle into the best selfish outcome; riders choose with a logit model; rides must at least cover fuel costs.

The comparison bar shows the same number of cars if everyone simply took the fastest road. Numbers illustrate the idea rather than reproduce the paper’s results.
